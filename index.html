Every green isa smalllandscape

Before a putt, a caddie reads three things: how far the ground falls, how fast it runs, and the line the ball will take.

The fall

Seen from the side, the 18th green is a shallow bowl. Its lowest point lies 5.6 metres below the back edge — exactly where the cup is cut.

The speed

The slope steepens toward the hollow, from under two per cent at the fringe to three and a half beside the hole. The ball gathers pace as it falls.

The line

Aim two cups high and let the land do the rest. The putt curls right, slows, and drops into the hollow.

Eighteen holesFour you will remember

Every hole at Fairhollow follows the fall of the land. Nothing was cut into the hill; the fairways simply lie where the ground lets them.

These four are where it shows most — kept in the yardage book the way the members keep them, worn at the corners from being read on the walk.
